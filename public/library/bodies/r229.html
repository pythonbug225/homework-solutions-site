Question 1. Let M and N be smooth manifolds and ϕ:M→N a smooth map.

•

Suppose X and Y are vector fields on M and N respectively. If Y is ϕ-related to X (i.e. Yϕ(p)​=(Dp​ϕ)(Xp​),∀p∈M), prove that (LY​f)∘ϕ=LX​(f∘ϕ),∀f∈C∞(N).

•

If X′ is ϕ-related to Y, prove that [X′,Y′] is ϕ-related to [X,Y].

Solution:

If Yϕ(p)​=(Dp​ϕ)Xp​,∀p∈M, then(LY​f)∘ϕ(p)​=(Dϕ(p)​f)(Yϕ(p)​)=(Dϕ(p)​f)(Dp​ϕ)(Xp​)=((Dp​ϕ)Xp​)(f)=LX​(f∘ϕ)(p).​

Hence X′Y′(f∘ϕ)=X′((Yf)∘ϕ)=(XYf)∘ϕ; similarly, Y′X′(f∘ϕ)=(YXf)∘ϕ. Therefore,[X′,Y′](f∘ϕ)​=X′Y′(f∘ϕ)−Y′X′(f∘ϕ)=(XYf)∘ϕ−(YXf)∘ϕ=([X,Y]f)∘ϕ.​

Question 2. Show that

•

GL(n,R) is a non-compact Lie group of n2 dimension;

•

GL(n,C) is a non-compact Lie group of 2n2 dimension;

•

both of them are non-abelian for n≥2.

Solution:

Let M(n,R) denote the set of n×n matrices with real entries. Because it is a real vector space of dimension n2 under matrix addition and scalar multiplication, M(n,R) is a smooth n2-dimensional smooth manifold. Now GL(n,R) is an open subset of M(n,R), namely the set where det:M(n,R)→R is nonzero, hence GL(n,R) is a smooth n2-dimensional submanifold of M(n,R). Moreover, for all x∈GL(n,R), λx∈GL(n,R)(λ∈R−{0}). Therefore GL(n,R) is not bounded, hence non-compact.

Next we prove that GL(n,R) admits a Lie group structure. Multiplication is smooth because the matrix entries of a product matrix AB are polynomials in the entries of A and B. Inversion is smooth by Cramer’s Rule. When n≥2, AB=BA, hence GL(n,R) is not Abelian when n≥2.

The case for GL(n,C) is similar.

Question 3. Find an explicit Lie group homomorphism ϕ:SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)→SU(5)with discrete kernel.

Solution:

Consider the homomorphismϕ:SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)→SU(5)(U3​,U2​,eiθ)↦(e−2iθU3​0​0e3iθU2​​).​

•

ϕ is indeed a Lie group homomorphism. ϕ is clearly a group homomorphism; to prove ϕ is smooth, note just ϕ(U3​,U2​,eiθ)=diag{e−2iθ,e−2iθ,e−2iθ,e3iθ,e3iθ}(U3​0​0U2​​).

The two terms of the left hand side of the above equation are embeddings of U(1) and SU(3)×SU(2) into SU(5), respectively. Hence they are smooth; but also product operation in Lie group is smooth, so ϕ is smooth.

•

The kernel of ϕ is discrete.

If (U3​,U2​,eiθ)∈Kerϕ, then U3​=diag{e2iθ,e2iθ,e2iθ}, U2​=diag{e−3iθ,e−3iθ}. Moreover, U3​∈SU(3), U2​∈SU(2) implies e6iθ=1, which means that the possible values of θ are taken from {3nπ​:n∈Z}, which is discrete.

Question 6. Let G be a connected Lie group. Define the centre of G as Z(G):={g∈G:gh=hg,∀h∈G}.Prove that

•

Z(G) is the kernel of the adjoint representation AdG​.

•

Z(G) is an embedded Lie subgroup in G with Lie algebra given by the centre z(g) of g.

•

g is abelian if and only if G is abelian.

•

AdG​ is trivial (i.e. every element of G is mapped to the identity) if and only if G is abelian.

•

The left-invariant and right-invariant vector fields on a connected Lie group G coincide if and only if G is abelian.

Solution: Suppose that Adg​ is trivial, and let h=exp(v)∈G. Now γ(t)=g⋅exp⋅g−1 is a curve in G whose velocity at t=0 is Adg​(v)=v, since Adg​ is trivial. However, ρ(t)=exp(tv) is also a curve in G with initial velocity v, and by the uniqueness of integral curve, g⋅exp(tv)⋅g−1=exp(tv). In particular, when t=1 one has gh=hg. For general h∈G, since G is connected, by Proposition 7.14 of [Lee13], h can always be written as h=exp(v1​)⋯exp(vk​), and the commutativity comes from iterating what we have done above. Hence Ker(Adg​)⊂Z(G). On the other hand elements of Z(G) clearly fall into Ker(AdG​). Therefore Ker(AdG​)=Z(G).

Now by Proposition 20.8 of [Lee13], we have a commutative diagramSince Ker(AdG​)=Z(G), the Lie algebra z(g) of Z(G) is given by Ker(adg​)={X∈g:[X,Y]=0,∀Y∈g}. In order this correspondence behaves well, one must demonstrate that Z(G) is a closed subgroup of G. For this, let Cg​={g1​∈G:g1​g=gg1​}. Then Cg​ is the preimage f−1({e}) where f is the smooth map f(g1​):=gg1​g−1g1−1​. Therefore, Cg​ is closed, and then Z(G)=∩g∈G​Cg​ is a closed subgroup of G.
