命题 0.4 (20.1.B). The intersection number is preserved by field extension of k.

Proof. Let p:XK​→X be the morphism induced by the field extension K/k. 命题 0.9 (20.1.3). Let X be a projective k-variety and fix a coherent sheaf F. Then the intersection number (L1​…Ln​⋅F) is a symmetric multilinear function of the line bundles L1​,…,Ln​.

注 0.10. The previous properties held without needing the assumption that dim(Supp F)≤n. However, starting with this multilinearity property, that dimension condition becomes essential.

注 0.11. Recall that L1​⊗L2​=O(D1​)⊗O(D2​)=O(D1​+D2​). Because of this, we can write the tensor product additively as L1​+L2​. The “addition” referred to in the multilinearity of the intersection number corresponds to this tensor product of line bundles.

Proof of 20.1.3. The symmetry of the intersection number is clear directly from the definition. Furthermore, by the base change property (20.1.B) 0.4, we may assume without loss of generality that the base field k is infinite.

We will prove the multilinearity by induction on n. Now we handle the base case for n=1 (20.1.E).

We have following observation: Let A be a very ample line bundle on X. This gives a closed immersion X↪P into a projective space P such that the pullback of O(1) is exactly A.

We can choose a hyperplane H of P such that the dimension of Supp(F)∩H drops by one, or dim(Supp(F)∩H)=0 (meaning it consists of a finite number of points).

Consider the standard short exact sequence:0→A∨→OX​→OX∩H​→0.I claim that we can choose a hyperplane H as above such that tensoring this sequence with F (i.e., applying the functor −⊗F) remains, in fact, exact.

Supplement to the previous claim: We only need the hyperplane H to avoid the associated primes Ass(F) of F. This is achievable because the set of associated primes is finite (∣Ass(F)∣<∞).

To verify the exactness, we only need to prove that the map Γ(U,F⊗A∨)→Γ(U,F) is injective for every affine open subset U⊂X.

Locally on an affine open U=Spec(R), let F be associated to an R-module M (so F∣U​=M), and let A∨ be represented by the ideal generated by a regular element r∈R (so A∨∣U​=rR). We need to show that the natural mapM⊗R​(rR)⟶M,m⊗rt⟼rtmis injective.

Suppose ∑(mi​⊗rti​)↦0. This means r⋅(∑mi​ti​)=0. Note that we can rewrite the element in the tensor product as ∑(mi​⊗rti​)=(∑mi​ti​)⊗r. To prove that ∑mi​ti​=0, it suffices to show that the multiplication map M⋅r​M is injective.

Recall from commutative algebra that the set of zero divisors of a module M is the union of its associated primes, ⋃p∈Ass(M)​p. If multiplication by r is not injective, then r must be a zero divisor. Consequently, there exists an associated prime p∈Ass(M) such that r∈p.

Geometrically, this implies that the point [p] lies in V(r)∩Supp(M), which corresponds to the intersection H∩Supp(F). However, this is a contradiction, since we explicitly chose the hyperplane H to avoid the associated primes Ass(F).

Therefore, the multiplication is injective, which implies that the sequence0⟶F⊗A∨⟶F⟶F⊗OX∩H​⟶0is indeed exact. Taking the Euler characteristic yields:χ(F)−χ(A∨⊗F)=χ(F∣X∩H​).

Because the support of F∣X∩H​ has dimension 0, tensoring it with any line bundle does not change its Euler characteristic, nor does it alter its support. Hence, for any arbitrary line bundle L, we have:χ(L∨⊗F)−χ(A∨⊗L∨⊗F)​=χ((L∨⊗F)∣X∩H​)=χ(F∣X∩H​)=χ(F)−χ(A∨⊗F).​This implies that the proposition holds when one of the line bundles is very ample, i.e.:(L1​⋅F)+(L2​⋅F)=((L1​⊗L2​)⋅F)holds when one of Li​ is very ample.

For arbitrary line bundles L1​ and L2​, there exist very ample line bundles Ai​ and Bi​ such that Li​≅Ai​⊗Bi∨​.

Since (OX​⋅F)=0, we have (Bi​⋅F)+(Bi∨​⋅F)=0, which implies (Bi∨​⋅F)=−(Bi​⋅F). Therefore, we can write:(Li​⋅F)=(Ai​⋅F)+(Bi∨​⋅F)=(Ai​⋅F)−(Bi​⋅F).By substituting these expressions and expanding, the multilinearity holds for the base case n=1.

Now, assume the proposition is correct for integers strictly less than n. We wish to show it holds for a coherent sheaf F∈Coh(X) with dim(Supp F)≤n. Specifically, we want to prove:(L1​L2​…Ln​⋅F)+(L1′​L2​…Ln​⋅F)=((L1​⊗L1′​)L2​…Ln​⋅F)(1)

Note that we can expand the intersection number of n+1 line bundles by partitioning the sum based on the inclusion of the first two indices:(L1​L1′​L2​…Ln​⋅F)​=excluding 1′∑​+excluding 1∑​+including both 1 and 1′∑​−excluding both 1 and 1′∑​=(L1​L2​…Ln​⋅F)+(L1′​L2​…Ln​⋅F)−((L1​⊗L1′​)L2​…Ln​⋅F).​This establishes the relation (20.1.F).

Next, suppose Ln​=O(D), where D is an effective Cartier divisor such that D∩Ass(F)=∅.

From the analysis above, tensoring the short exact sequence0⟶O(−D)=Ln∨​⟶OX​⟶OD​⟶0with Li1​∨​⊗⋯⊗Lim​∨​⊗F preserves exactness. 定理 0.16 (Asymptotic Riemann-Roch, 20.1.I). Let X be a projective variety, and let F∈Coh(X) be a coherent sheaf such that dim(Supp F)≤n. Let L be a line bundle on X. Then the function defined byq(m):=χ(X,L⊗m⊗F)is a polynomial in m of degree at most n, and the coefficient of the mn term is given by n!1​(Ln⋅F).

Proof. By Corollary 20.1.H, since we are intersecting n+1 line bundles and the dimension of the support of L⊗m⊗F is at most n, the intersection number vanishes:(n+1LL…L​​⋅(L⊗m⊗F))=0.By the definition of the intersection number and the multilinearity, we obtain:j=0∑n+1​(−1)n+1−j(jn+1​)χ(X,L⊗(m+j)⊗F)=j=0∑n+1​(−1)n+1−j(jn+1​)q(m+j)=0for all integers m∈Z. In fact, this alternating sum represents the (n+1)-th finite difference of the function q(m), commonly denoted as Δn+1q(m).

Since Δn+1q(m)=0 for all m, we conclude that q(m) must be a polynomial in m of degree at most n.

Let us write this polynomial explicitly as q(m)=∑i=0n​ci​mi. A standard property of the finite difference operator states that the n-th finite difference of a polynomial of degree n is constant and is given by Δnq(m)=n!⋅cn​.

Now, applying the definition of the intersection number again for n copies of the line bundle L, we find that:(nLL…L​​⋅(L⊗m⊗F))=Δnq(m).Evaluating it at m=0 gives (Ln⋅F). Equating this with the n-th finite difference yields:(Ln⋅F)=n!⋅cn​.Therefore, the leading coefficient of the polynomial is precisely cn​=n!1​(Ln⋅F), which completes the proof.

□

推论 0.19 (20.1.J). π:X1​⟶X2​, L1​,…,Ln​ as above. Then we have: (π∗L1​…π∗Ln​)=deg(X1​/X2​)⋅(L1​…Ln​).

Proof Sketch. From the general projection formula, we have:(L1​L2​…Ln​⋅π∗​OX1​​)=(π∗L1​…π∗Ln​⋅OX1​​).Thus, we need to show that:(L1​L2​…Ln​⋅π∗​OX1​​)=deg(X1​/X2​)⋅(L1​…Ln​⋅OX2​​).

Case 1: Suppose π is not dominant.
The right-hand side is trivially 0 because deg(X1​/X2​)=0. For the left-hand side, since π is a projective morphism, its image is a closed subset, which means Supp(π∗​OX1​​)=π(X1​)​=π(X1​). Because π is not dominant, we have π(X1​)⊊X2​, which implies that dim(Supp(π∗​OX1​​))<n. Therefore, intersecting n line bundles with it yields 0, so the left-hand side is also 0.

Case 2: Suppose π is dominant.
Since both the left-hand side and the right-hand side are multilinear, it suffices to prove the case when the line bundles L1​,…,Ln​ are all very ample.

(i) We can choose an open subset U′⊆X2​ such that the restriction π∣π−1(U′)​ is finite (cf. 10.3.G). •

(D1​∩⋯∩Dn​)∩Z=∅,

•

命题 0.20 (20.1.K). Let X be a projective variety, and let Y be a subvariety of X of dimension n. If L1​,…,Ln​ are ample line bundles on X, then we have:(L1​…Ln​⋅Y)>0.

Proof. We proceed by induction on n.

For the base case n=1, Y is a projective curve. We know that a line bundle on a curve is ample if and only if its degree is strictly positive. Therefore, the intersection number is simply the degree, which gives:(L1​⋅Y)=degY​(L1​∣Y​)>0.

Now, assume the proposition holds for dimension n−1. Since Ln​ is ample, there exists a sufficiently large integer m>0 such that the tensor power Ln⊗m​ becomes very ample.

By the multilinearity of the intersection number, we can write:m(L1​L2​…Ln​⋅Y)=(L1​L2​…Ln−1​Ln⊗m​⋅Y).Because Ln⊗m​ is very ample, we can choose an effective Cartier divisor D such that Ln⊗m​≅O(D) and D intersects Y properly. Then the intersection number becomes:(L1​L2​…Ln−1​O(D)⋅Y)=(L1​L2​…Ln−1​⋅D∩Y).

Notice that D∩Y is a closed subscheme of Y of dimension n−1. The inclusion map D∩Y↪Y is a finite morphism (specifically, a closed immersion). Since the pullback via a finite morphism preserves the ampleness of line bundles, the restrictions of L1​,…,Ln−1​ to D∩Y are still ample line bundles.

By our induction hypothesis on the (n−1)-dimensional variety D∩Y, we have:(L1​L2​…Ln−1​⋅D∩Y)>0.Since m>0, this immediately implies that (L1​…Ln​⋅Y)>0, which completes the proof.
