引理 0.1 (20.2.I). X is a scheme, V is a rank 2 locally free sheaf on X. PV:=ProjOX​​(Sym∙V∨). σ:X→PV is a section of the projection π:PV→X. Then the normal bundle Nσ(X)/PV​:=σ∗OPV​(σ(X)) is isomorphic to L⊗2⊗detV, where L:=σ∗OPV​(1).

注 0.2. In the original description in [FOAG] 20.2.I, by 17.2.I, one section always corresponds to an exact sequence 0→S→V→Q→0. S corresponds to L∨, S∨⊗Q=S∨⊗2⊗(S⊗Q)=S∨⊗2⊗detV corresponds to L⊗2⊗detV here. We will not follow the idea in [FOAG]. If you first read it, you can skip and acknowledge it.

Proof. 1. Setup and Trivialization
Choose an affine open cover X=⋃Ui​ where Ui​=SpecAi​, such that the cover is subordinate to both V∨ and L.

•

Over Ui​, V∨ is trivialized with basis (si0∨​,si1∨​). On the overlap Ui​∩Uj​, the transition matrix is Mij​=(AC​BD​). Thus: si0∨​si1∨​​=Asj0∨​+Csj1∨​=Bsj0∨​+Dsj1∨​​The transition function for detV∨ is det(Mij​)=AD−BC.

•

Over Ui​, L is trivialized with basis ei​. On the overlap, ei​=cij​ej​, meaning the transition function for L is cij​.

2. The Section and its Compatibility
Locally over Ui​, this acts on the basis as:si0∨​↦bi0​ei​,si1∨​↦bi1​ei​To be compatible with the transition functions on Ui​∩Uj​, substituting ei​=cij​ej​ yields the relations for the coefficients:cij​bi0​cij​bi1​​=Abj0​+Cbj1​=Bbj0​+Dbj1​​Because OPV​(1)∣D+​(si0∨​)​=Ai​[si0∨​si1∨​​]⋅si0∨​​, σ∗si0∨​ is also a generator of L∣σ−1(D+​(si0∨​)​, hence bi0​ is a unit in Γ(σ−1(D+​(si0∨​)),OUi​​). 3. The Homogeneous Ideal of the Section

Expanding cij​Fi​:cij​Fi​=(cij​bi1​)si0∨​−(cij​bi0​)si1∨​Substitute the transition relations for s∨ and b:cij​Fi​=(Bbj0​+Dbj1​)(Asj0∨​+Csj1∨​)−(Abj0​+Cbj1​)(Bsj0∨​+Dsj1∨​)Expanding and canceling the AB and CD terms leaves:cij​Fi​=(AD−BC)(bj1​sj0∨​−bj0​sj1∨​)=det(Mij​)Fj​Thus, the transition relation for the homogeneous generator is:Fi​=cij−1​det(Mij​)Fj​

4. Local Affine Generators of the Conormal Bundle
To find the conormal bundle I/I2, we must pass to affine charts.

•

In the standard affine chart D+​(si0∨​)≅SpecAi​[si0∨​si1∨​​], we divide Fi​ by the invertible element si0∨​ to get the local generator: Υi0​=si0∨​Fi​​=bi1​−bi0​si0∨​si1∨​​

•

In the other affine chart D+​(si1∨​)≅SpecAi​[si1∨​si0∨​​], we divide by si1∨​: Υi1​=si1∨​Fi​​=bi1​si1∨​si0∨​​−bi0​

5. Gluing the Global Generator over SpecAi​
We pull these generators back to the base space X via σ∗. Since σ∗(si0∨​)=bi0​ and σ∗(si1∨​)=bi1​, the relation becomes:σ∗Υi0​=bi0​bi1​​σ∗Υi1​Cross-multiplying eliminates the fractions:bi0​σ∗Υi0​=bi1​σ∗Υi1​Let us define this common element as Φi​. Γ(σ−1(D+​(si1∨​)),OUi​​)), bi0​σ∗Υi0​ and bi1​σ∗Υi1​ are both generators of the conormal bundle over their respective affine charts. Now we determine how Φi​ transforms to Φj​ on Ui​∩Uj​. •

•

定义 0.3 (Hirzebruch surface). We define the Hirzebruch surface Fn​ as the projective bundle Fn​:=P(OP1​⊕OP1​(n))equipped with the natural projection π:Fn​→P1.

Consider a section σ:P1→Fn​ such that σ∗OFn​​(1)=OP1​(−n), which yields the following natural short exact sequence:0→OP1​(n)→OP1​⊕OP1​(n)→OP1​→0(The existence follows from 17.2.I.)

Let N denote the normal bundle of this section. By applying the previously established isomorphism for normal bundles of sections in projective bundles, we find that:N≅OP1​(−n)We define the curve E⊆Fn​ to be the image of this section, namely E:=σ(P1). The self-intersection number of the curve E on the surface Fn​ is given by the degree of its normal bundle. Therefore, we obtain:(E⋅E)=−n

Let us consider another section σ′:P1→Fn​ such that σ′∗OFn​​(1)=OP1​. This corresponds to the following exact sequence:0→OP1​→OP1​⊕OP1​(n)→OP1​(n)→0Let C denote the image of this section. Its self-intersection number is given by:(C⋅C)=deg(OP1∨​⊗OP1​(n))=n

Let p be any k-point of P1, and let F=π−1(p) denote the fiber over p.

There is an observation (20.2.K) that: The line bundle O(F) is independent of the choice of the point p.

Proof of the observation. Since the projection morphism π is flat, the pullback of the Weil divisor π∗{p} is well-defined1. We have O(F)=O(π∗{p})≅π∗OP1​({p})≅π∗OP1​(1). This shows that O(F) depends only on the degree of the point, making it independent of the specific choice of p.

□

命题 0.4 (20.2.L). The Picard group Pic(Fn​) is generated by the classes of E and F.

Proof Sketch. We outline the idea of the proof. The base P1 can be covered by two affine open patches Speck[x] and Speck[y], glued together via x↔y−1. Over Speck[x], the projective bundle is locally given by Projk[x][Z0​,Z1​], where Z0​ corresponds to the dual of 1∈Γ(Speck[x],OP1​) and Z1​ corresponds to the dual of a local generator in Γ(Speck[x],OP1​(n)).

We can restrict our attention to an affine open subscheme Speck[x,Z1​/Z0​]⊆Projk[x][Z0​,Z1​]⊆Fn​. The closed subset defined by V+​(Z0​) corresponds to the subbundle OP1​(n)↪OP1​⊕OP1​(n), which is exactly the negative section E.

Note that the complement of this affine chart in Fn​, namely Fn​∖Speck[x,Z1​/Z0​], consists of E=V+​(Z0​) union the preimage under π of the point at infinity of P1 (which corresponds to the origin in Speck[y]). This preimage is simply a fiber F.

Since the Picard group of the affine space Speck[x,Z0​/Z1​] is trivial, the excision exact sequence implies that Pic(Fn​) is generated by the classes of the irreducible components of the complement, which are E and F. By a similar argument, the Picard group is also generated by C and F.

□

Now let’s calculate (20.2.M) the intersection numbers for these classes.

By choosing two distinct points p,q∈P1 (for example, the origin of Speck[x] and the origin of Speck[y]), their corresponding fibers Fp​ and Fq​ are disjoint, meaning Fp​∩Fq​=∅. Therefore, the self-intersection of the fiber class is zero:(F⋅F)=(Fp​⋅Fq​)=0

From previous results, we already know that the self-intersection of the negative section is:(E⋅E)=−n

Finally, since the embedding E↪Fn​ acts as a section of the projection π, it intersects every fiber in exactly one point. Therefore, the intersection number is:(E⋅F)=#(E∩F)=1This intersection corresponds to a single point on E≅P1.

Since the Picard group is generated by E and F, by linear independence gained by the computation above, we have an isomorphism Pic(Fn​)≅Z[E]⊕Z[F]. Thus, we can write the class of the curve C as:[C]=a[E]+b[F]Using the intersection numbers, we can solve for a and b:(C⋅F)(C⋅C)​=1⟹a(E⋅F)+b(F⋅F)=a=1=n⟹a2(E⋅E)+2ab(E⋅F)+b2(F⋅F)=1(−n)+2b(1)=n⟹b=n​Therefore, the class of C is [C]=[E]+n[F].

推论 0.5 (20.2.O). The dimension of the space of global sections satisfies h0(Fn​,OFn​​(C))>1.

Proof. This follows because C is an effective curve with linear equivalence class C∼E+nF.

命题 0.6 (20.2.P). Every effective curve on Fn​ is a non-negative linear combination of E and F. That is, for every effective curve D, there exist non-negative integers a,b∈Z≥0​ such that D∼aE+bF.

Proof. It suffices to show the case when D is irreducible.

If there exists a closed point p such that D=π−1(p), then D∼F and we are done.

Otherwise, since Pic(Fn​)≅Z[E]⊕Z[F], we can write the class of D as D∼aE+bF. Because D is not a fiber, its intersection with a general fiber F is non-negative:(D⋅F)≥0⟹a(E⋅F)+b(F⋅F)=a≥0By Corollary 20.2.O, we can find another effective curve C′∼C different from D, hence such that (D⋅C′)≥0. Since C′ is linearly equivalent to C, we have (D⋅C)≥0. Calculating this intersection yields:(D⋅C)=(aE+bF)⋅(E+nF)=a(−n)+an+b=bThus, we conclude that b≥0.

□

推论 0.7 (20.2.Q). The Hirzebruch surfaces Fn​ are pairwise non-isomorphic.

Proof. Suppose there exists an isomorphism ψ:Fn​≅​Fm​.

We know the structure of their respective Picard groups and intersection pairings:

•

Pic(Fn​)=Z[En​]⊕Z[Fn​] with (En​⋅En​)=−n, (En​⋅Fn​)=1, and (Fn​⋅Fn​)=0.

•

Pic(Fm​)=Z[Em​]⊕Z[Fm​] with (Em​⋅Em​)=−m, (Em​⋅Fm​)=1, and (Fm​⋅Fm​)=0.

The isomorphism ψ induces an invertible linear map on the Picard groups:[ψ∗Em​ψ∗Fm​​]=[ac​bd​][En​Fn​​]and its inverse map gives:[(ψ−1)∗En​(ψ−1)∗Fn​​]=[d−c​−ba​][Em​Fm​​]where the determinant ad−bc=1.

By Proposition 20.2.P, isomorphisms map effective curves to effective curves, so all coefficients in both transformation matrices must be non-negative. Therefore, we must have a,b,c,d≥0 as well as −b≥0 and −c≥0.

This implies b=c=0. Since ad−bc=1 and a,d≥0, it follows that a=d=1.

Thus, ψ∗Em​=En​. Since isomorphisms preserve intersection numbers, we have:(Em​⋅Em​)=(ψ∗Em​⋅ψ∗Em​)=(En​⋅En​)Substituting the self-intersection values yields −m=−n, which forces m=n.

□

命题 0.8 (20.2.S). The nef cone of the Hirzebruch surface Fn​, denoted Nef(Fn​), is generated by the classes C and F.

Proof. By the definition of a nef divisor, a divisor class D is nef if and only if its intersection number with every irreducible curve I is non-negative, that is, (D⋅I)≥0.

By Proposition 20.2.P, every effective curve (and thus every irreducible curve) I can be expressed as a non-negative linear combination of E and F.

Therefore, by linearity of the intersection product, to check if a divisor D is nef, it suffices to check its intersection with the base generators E and F. Thus, D is nef if and only if:(D⋅E)≥0and(D⋅F)≥0

Since the Picard group Pic(Fn​) is generated by E and F, and we know that C∼E+nF, the classes C and F also form a basis. We can therefore write any divisor class D as a linear combination of C and F:D∼aC+bFwhich is linearly equivalent to aE+(b+an)F.

Let us compute the intersection numbers of D with F and E:(D⋅F)(D⋅E)​=(aC+bF)⋅F=a(C⋅F)+b(F⋅F)=a(1)+0=a=(aC+bF)⋅E=a(C⋅E)+b(F⋅E)=a(0)+b(1)=b​(Note that we used the relation (C⋅E)=(E+nF)⋅E=(E⋅E)+n(F⋅E)=−n+n(1)=0).

Consequently, the nef conditions (D⋅F)≥0 and (D⋅E)≥0 are exactly equivalent to:a≥0andb≥0

This proves that a divisor D is nef if and only if it is a non-negative linear combination of C and F. Hence, the nef cone is generated by C and F.
