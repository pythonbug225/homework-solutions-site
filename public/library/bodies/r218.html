1Set up for sheaf theory

There are four common settings for the study of sheaves in algebraic geometry and representation theory:

1.

Betti setting: We consider complex analytic spaces endowed with the classical topology (not the Zariski topology).
Note: We will exclusively focus on this setting for the duration of this course.

2.

Étale setting over C: We consider complex varieties equipped with the étale topology, using l-adic coefficients. This topology acts as a remedy for the topological shortcomings of the Zariski topology.

3.

Algebraic (de Rham) setting: We consider complex varieties endowed with the Zariski topology. This setting heavily relies on the theory of D-modules (which behave significantly differently from standard coherent sheaves).

4.

Positive characteristic setting: We consider varieties defined over a finite field Fq​, equipped with the étale topology and using l-adic coefficients.

Relations:

•

Why not use C-sheaves on the Zariski topology?

引理 1.1. Any locally constant sheaf on an irreducible topological space is constant.

注 1.2. The Zariski topology has too few open sets, making it inconvenient to use sheaves to study topological properties.

2Local Systems

定义 2.1. A local system (of C-vector spaces) on a topological space X is a locally constant sheaf. i.e., ∀x∈X, ∃ open x∈U⊂X such that F∣U​ is constant.

例 2.2. Let X=C∖{0}, for any U⊂X:F(U)={holomorphic functions f on U∣∂z​f=2z1​f}then F is a local system and we can find Fx​≅Cz1/2∀x∈X.
But z1/2 can’t be defined on X, so F is not a constant sheaf.

引理 2.3. Any local system on [0,1] is constant.

Proof. Let L be a local system on [0,1] (compact). There exists a finite open covering [0,1]=⋃i​Ui​ such that L∣Ui​​ is constant. We may assume the finite open covering consists of intervals Ui​=(ai​,bi​) (or half-open intervals like [0,bi​) and (ai​,1]) such that bi​∈Ui+1​. For example, let U1​=[0,b1​) and U2​=(a2​,b2​), so that their intersection is U1​∩U2​=(a2​,b1​), and so forth. We consider the restriction maps:L(U1​)≅​L(U1​∩U2​)≅​L(U2​).This implies that the local system L is constant on the union U1​∪U2​. By continuing this process with induction, we conclude that L is constant on the entire interval [0,1].

□

Similarly, any local system on [0,1]×[0,1] is constant.

引理 2.4. For any continuous map f:X→Y and a local system L on Y, the pullback f∗L is a local system on X.

Proof. Locally, the pullback functor f∗ sends a constant sheaf to a constant sheaf.

□

3Monodromy representations

Let Loc(X) denote the category of local systems on X.

命题 3.1. For a locally connected topological space X, Loc(X) is an abelian category.

Fix a base point x0​∈X. Our goal is to construct a functorLoc(X)⟶Rep(π1​(X,x0​))where Rep(π1​(X,x0​)) represents the category of finite dimensional representations {(V,π1​(X,x0​)→GL(V))}.

For any path γ:[0,1]→X and any local system L∈Loc(X), note that the pullback γ∗L is constant on [0,1] by the previous lemma. This gives us a natural isomorphism ρ(γ) through the global sections Γ([0,1],γ∗L). Specifically, we have the following identifications of stalks:Lγ(1)​=(γ∗L)1​≅​Γ([0,1],γ∗L)≅​(γ∗L)0​=Lγ(0)​.For a loop where γ(0)=γ(1)=x0​, we obtain an isomorphismρ(γ):Lx0​​≅​Lx0​​.That is, ρ(γ)∈GL(Lx0​​).

引理 3.2. The assignment ρ(γ) induces a well-defined action of the fundamental group π1​(X,x0​) on the vector space V=Lx0​​.

Proof. To show that this action is well-defined, we need to verify the following properties:

1.

If γ is the identity path, then ρ(γ) is the identity isomorphism.

2.

The assignment respects path composition.

3.

The assignment is invariant under homotopy. That is, if two paths are homotopic (γ∼γ′), then their induced isomorphisms are equal, meaning ρ(γ)=ρ(γ′).

To prove the homotopy invariance, let H:[0,1]×[0,1]→X be a homotopy between γ and γ′, such that H(t,0)=γ(t) and H(t,1)=γ′(t). Since the pullback H∗L is a constant local system on the square [0,1]×[0,1], its restriction to the boundary paths is also constant. This yields γ∗L=γ′∗L, which implies that ρ(γ)=ρ(γ′).

□

We have thus defined the monodromy representation functor:Monx0​​:Loc(X)L​⟶Rep(π1​(X,x0​))⟼Lx0​​.​

例 3.3. Consider the previous example of the local system associated with z1/2 on X=C∖{0}. Let the base point be x0​=1. The fundamental group is π1​(X,x0​)≅Z. The monodromy representation maps the generator of the fundamental group to an element in GL(C)≅C∗. Specifically, as z goes around the origin once, the branch of z1/2 changes sign, so the generator 1∈Z is mapped to −1∈C∗ (i.e., z1/2↦−z1/2).

定理 3.4. Suppose the topological space X is connected, locally path-connected, and locally simply-connected. Then the monodromy functor Monx0​​ is an equivalence of categories.

注 3.5. For example, topological manifolds satisfy these local conditions. In addition, cones also satisfy these local conditions.

Fact: For the inverse functor, Let p:X~→X be the universal covering space. The fundamental group π1​(X,x0​) acts on X~ via deck transformations on the fibers of p.

Furthermore, we have an isomorphism on the stalk at the base point: (LV​)x0​​≅V. And for any local system L, we have LLx0​​​≅L. (Hint: Fix a point x~0​∈p−1(x0​). For any x∈X, fix a path αx​ from x0​ to x (fixing this data ensures consistency), with the condition that the chosen path for the base point αx0​​ is the identity path.)

引理 3.6. Let X be as in the previous theorem (which implies X is path-connected). For any local system L∈Loc(X) corresponding to a representation V∈Rep(π1​(X,x0​)), there is an isomorphism of global sections:Γ(X,L)≅Vπ1​(X,x0​).

Proof. We have a natural evaluation map Γ(X,L)→Lx0​​=V.

For any loop γ∈π1​(X,x0​) where γ(0)=γ(1)=x0​, the monodromy action is given by the composition of isomorphisms:ρ(γ):V=Lx0​​≅​Γ([0,1],γ∗L)≅​Lx0​​.For any global section s∈Γ(X,L), its pullback along the loop yields s∣[0,1]​∈Γ([0,1],γ∗L). This implies that ρ(γ)(sx0​​)=sx0​​. Thus, the image of the evaluation map lies in the invariant subspace Vπ1​(X,x0​).

To show this map is an isomorphism, we prove injectivity and surjectivity separately:

•

Injectivity: For any two global sections s,s′∈Γ(X,L), suppose their stalks at x0​ are equal, meaning sx0​​=sx0​′​. For any arbitrary point x∈X, take a path α such that α(0)=x0​ and α(1)=x. This path induces an isomorphism ρ(α):Lx0​​≅​Lx​. Because s and s′ are global sections, we have ρ(α)(sx0​​)=sx​ and ρ(α)(sx0​′​)=sx′​. Given that sx0​​=sx0​′​, it follows immediately that sx​=sx′​. Since x is arbitrary, we conclude that s=s′.

•

Surjectivity: Let v∈Vπ1​(X,x0​) be an invariant element. For any x∈X, take a path α from x0​ to x. This path induces an isomorphism ρ(α):Lx0​​≅​Lx​. We can define the stalk at x by setting vx​:=ρ(α)(v). Because v is invariant under the action of the fundamental group π1​(X,x0​), this definition is independent of the choice of the path α. This process yields a well-defined global section s∈Γ(X,L) such that sx0​​=v.

□

4Constructible Sheaves

It is not enough to consider the category of local systems Loc(X) for the following reasons:

1.

It only detects the fundamental group π1​(X,x0​).

2.

It is not stable under functors.

例 4.1. Let L be the local system associated with z1/2 on X=C∖{0}. Consider the inclusion map j:X↪Y=C, and the pushforward sheaf j∗​L on Y. Let us compute the stalk at the origin:(j∗​L)0​=0∈V⊆Ylim​​(j∗​L)(V)=0∈Vlim​​L(V∖{0})=0.Since the stalk at 0 is zero but the stalks elsewhere are non-zero, the sheaf j∗​L is not a local system on Y. Thus, j∗​L∈/Loc(Y).

定义 4.2. A stratification of a topological space X is a finite collection of subspaces (Xi​)i∈I​ of X, called strata, such that:

1.

X=⋃i∈I​Xi​.

2.

Xi​∩Xj​=∅ for any i=j.

3.

Every stratum Xi​ is a connected topological manifold.

4.

Every stratum Xi​ is locally closed in X.

5.

If Xi​∩Xj​​=∅, then Xi​⊆Xj​​. (This is equivalent to the frontier condition: the boundary ∂Xi​=Xi​​∖Xi​ is a union of some strata Xj​).

注 4.3. When X is an algebraic variety, we require that each stratum Xi​ is a smooth, connected, Zariski locally closed subvariety.

定义 4.4. A refinement of a stratification X=⨆i∈I​Xi​ is another stratification X=⨆j∈J​Xj​ such that every stratum Xj​ is contained in some stratum Xi​.

例 4.5.

1.

If X is a manifold, then it has a trivial stratification X=X1​.

2.

Let X={(x,y)∈C2∣xy=0}⊂C2. We can stratify X as: X={0}⊔(X∖{0})={0}⊔(x-axis∖{0})⊔(y-axis∖{0}).

3.

Let G be an algebraic group (for example, GLn​(C)) and X be an algebraic variety equipped with a G-action that has finitely many orbits. This naturally induces a stratification X=⨆(G-orbits).

As a specific case, consider the general linear group GLn​(C) acting by conjugation on the nilpotent cone N={A∈Mn×n​(C)∣An=0}. The set of orbits ∣GLn​(C)∖N∣ is in bijection with the set of partitions of n.

引理 4.6. Let X be an algebraic variety.

1.

Any two stratifications of X have a common refinement.

2.

For any locally closed subvariety Y⊆X, Y is a union of strata for some stratification of X.

Proof. (1) This is left as a homework exercise. The general strategy is to consider the smooth locus of the variety and proceed using Noetherian induction.

(2) The proof follows a similar idea.

□

注 4.7. In more general topological settings, there exist certain regularity conditions one might need to impose on a stratification, such as the Whitney stratification conditions. However, we can safely avoid these technical conditions when we are working exclusively with algebraic varieties.

定义 4.8. Let X be a topological space equipped with a stratification (Xi​)i∈I​.

A sheaf F on X is weakly constructible with respect to I if its restriction F∣Xi​​∈Loc(Xi​) for all i∈I. A sheaf is simply called weakly constructible if it is weakly constructible with respect to some stratification I.

Furthermore, a sheaf is constructible (with respect to I) if, moreover, for all i∈I, the restriction F∣Xi​​ is of finite rank (i.e., all of its stalks are finite-dimensional vector spaces).

We denote the category of constructible sheaves on X (with respect to a fixed stratification I) by Shc​(X) (respectively, ShI​(X)).

例 4.9.

1.

If X is a smooth variety, then the category of finite-rank local systems Locfin(X) is a subcategory of Shc​(X).

2.

For the local system L associated with z1/2 on C∖{0}, let j:C∖{0}↪C be the inclusion map. Then the pushforward sheaf j∗​L belongs to Shc​(X) (where X=C).

On derived categories:

定义 4.10. A complex of sheaves F∙ is called (weakly) constructible (with respect to I) if its k-th cohomology sheaf Hk(F∙) satisfies the same property for all k∈Z.

We denote the corresponding derived categories as DIb​(X)⊆Db(X) and Dcb​(X)⊆Db(X), where Db(X) stands for the bounded derived category of sheaves on X. Both DIb​(X) and Dcb​(X) form full triangulated subcategories.

There is a natural functor connecting the derived category of constructible sheaves to the constructible derived category:Db(Shc​(X))⟶Dcb​(X).

定理 4.11. For any complex algebraic variety X, the natural functor from the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves to the constructible derived category, Db(Shc​(X))≃​Dcb​(X),is an equivalence of categories.

Proof. It can be found in Beilinson’s foundational notes, specifically in the work titled “On the derived category of perverse sheaves”.

□

注 4.12. It is important to note that this equivalence generally fails for complex analytic spaces. However, interestingly, the equivalence still holds for real analytic spaces.
