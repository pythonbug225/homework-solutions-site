3Differentiation of measures

4Fourier Transforms

4.1

1.

A measure is called continuous if each point has measure zero.

Suppose {μN​},N=1,2…, and ν are non-negative Radon measures on R, and that ν is continuous. Assume that μ​N​(ξ)→ν(ξ) as N→∞, for each ξ∈R. Then μN​((a,b))→ν((a,b)) for all a<b.

5Self–similar sets

The main references of this chapter are [Hochman; Lectures on fractal geometry and dynamics, Chapter 5], [Bishop, Peres; Fractals in Probability and Analysis, Chapter 2], [Mattila; Fourier analysis and Hausdorff dimension, Chapter 9], and [Shen; Lectures on Bernoulli Convolution].

5.1

1.

Let 0<ε<δ. Show that A(ε)⊂A(δ), where A(ε):={x∈X:d(x,A)<ε}.

2.

Let (X,d) be a metric space, and E⊂X. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

E is complete and totally bounded.

(ii)

Every sequence in E has a subsequence convergent to a point in E.

(iii)

E is compact.

and (ii) ℓj′​:=ℓj​∩B(0,2) is convergent under the Hausdorff metric.

3.

Suppose (X,A,μ) is a measurable space, and we call A∈A a μ-atom if μ(A)>0, and for any B∈A with B⊂A we have either μ(B)=0 or μ(A∖B)=0. Show that
