REPRESENTATION THEORY: MIDTERM EXAM

In what follows, k is always a field and G is always a finite group. By a representation, we always mean a linear representation of G over k.

1.

True or False? (10 points) Not necessary to give a proof or a counterexample.

(1)

Suppose that p:=char(k) divides ∣G∣. Then a representation V is irreducible if and only if its dual V∗ is irreducible.

(2)

Suppose that char(k)=0. A representation V is irreducible if and only if for any finite extension k′/k, the scalar extension V⊗k​k′ is irreducible.

(3)

Let V be a representation over C, then V≅V∗ if and only if χV​∈CF(G,R).

(4)

Let X be a finite G-set and kX be the induced linear representation of G. Then X is irreducible if and only if kX is irreducible.

(5)

The tensor product V⊗k​W of two irreducible representations V and W is also irreducible.

2.

(10 points) Let X be a G-set. Show that the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

X is indecomposable.

(b)

X is irreducible.

(c)

X is transitive.

3.

(15 points) Suppose that char(k)=0. Show that if V is a linear representation of G, then for any sub-representation W⊂V, there exists another sub-representation W⊥⊂V such that V=W⊕W⊥.

4.

(20 points) Suppose that char(k)=0. For any representation V, put DV​:=EndG​(V).

(1)

Show that V is irreducible if and only if DV​ is a division algebra.

(2)

Show that there exists an isomorphism of k-algebras ρG​:kG→∏V∈Irrk​(G)​EndDV​​(V).

5.

(20 points) Let G=Z/nZ be the cyclic group of order n≥3. Find all irreducible representations of G over R.

6.

(10 points) Suppose that (V,ρ) is a faithful irreducible linear representation of G over C. Show that the center Z(G) of G is a cyclic group.

7.

(15 points) For any representation W of G over C, it defines a representation WR​ of G over R by regarding W as a vector space over R. Let V be an irreducible representation of G over R. Show that:

(1)

The morphism of C-algebras ι:C⊗R​EndG​(V)→EndG​(C⊗R​V),λ⊗ϕ↦λϕis an isomorphism.

(2)

C⊗R​V is irreducible if and only if ⟨χV​,χV​⟩G​=1; in this case, EndG​(V)≅R.

(3)

If C⊗R​V is not irreducible, then there is an irreducible representation W over C such that C⊗R​V≅W⊕W∗ and WR​≅V.

(i)

If W is not isomorphic to W∗, show that EndG​(V)≅C.

(ii)

If W≅W∗, show that W is not R-rational and dimR​EndG​(V)=4.
