1Open-closed distinguished triangle

Let i:Z↪X be a closed subset, and let j:U=X∖Z↪X be the open complement. We have the relations:i∗​=i!​andj∗=j!

定理 1.1. For the inclusions i:Z↪X and j:U↪X, the following hold:

1.

i∗j!​=i!j∗​=j∗i∗​=0.

2.

For any sheaf F∈D(X), there exists a natural distinguished triangle: j!​j∗F⟶F⟶i∗​i∗F+1​

3.

For any sheaf F∈D(X), there exists a natural distinguished triangle: i∗​i!F⟶F⟶j∗​j∗F+1​

注 1.2. Notice that the first maps in the triangles essentially use "!", while the second maps use "∗". This pattern works because i∗​=i!​ and j∗=j!.

Proof. It suffices to show these properties in the abelian category of sheaves.

For (1), consider the stalks of the sheaves. For a sheaf F, the stalk (j!​F)x​=0 for x∈/U. Similarly, (i!​F)x​=0 for x∈/Z. This immediately implies that:i∗j!​=j∗i∗​=0By Verdier duality, the functor i!∘j∗​ is the right adjoint to the functor j∗∘i∗​=0. Because the right adjoint of the zero functor is also zero, it follows that i!∘j∗​=0.

For (2), we utilize the properties of adjunction. From the adjunction isomorphism Hom(j∗F,j∗F)≅Hom(j!​j∗F,F), the identity map on the left induces a natural counit map:j!​j∗F⟶FThe natural unit map F⟶i∗​i∗F can also be obtained in a similar manner.

In abelian categories, for a sheaf F∈Sh(X), we can verify exactness by checking the stalks. Consider the sequence of stalks:(j!​j∗F)x​⟶Fx​⟶(i∗​i∗F)x​(*)(1)For x∈U, we have (j!​j∗F)x​=Fx​ and (i∗​i∗F)x​=0. For x∈/U, we have (j!​j∗F)x​=0 and (i∗​i∗F)x​=Fx​. In both cases, the sequence (1) is exact. This exact sequence in the abelian category induces the corresponding distinguished triangle in the derived category.

For (3), first we consider the abelian category. By definition, for any open set V, the sections of the pushforward of the pullback are given by:(j∗​j∗F)(V)=F(V∩U)The kernel of the unit map F⟶j∗​j∗F evaluated on V is:ker(F⟶j∗​j∗F)(V)​={s∈F(V)∣s∣V∩U​=0}={s∈F(V)∣supps⊆V∩Z}​Since Z is a closed subset (i.e., Z=Z), this coincides with the sections with supports in Z, namely (i!F)(V∩Z). Therefore, the following sequence is exact:0⟶i∗​i!F⟶F⟶j∗​j∗F

To extend this to the derived category, we take an injective resolution of F. Recall that injective sheaves are flasque. For an injective (and hence flasque) sheaf I, the restriction map is surjective:I(V)⟶(j∗​j∗I)(V)=I(V∩U) is surjective.

To properly extend this to the derived category D(X), we replace F with its injective resolution I∙. Since a sheaf and its injective resolution are quasi-isomorphic, they are isomorphic in D(X).

Furthermore, because injective sheaves are flasque, the restriction map I∙⟶j∗​j∗I∙ is surjective in every degree. This elevates our previous left-exact sequence to a short exact sequence of complexes:0⟶i∗​i!I∙⟶I∙⟶j∗​j∗I∙⟶0A short exact sequence of complexes naturally induces a distinguished triangle in the derived category, yielding i∗​i!F⟶F⟶j∗​j∗F+1​. This completes the proof.

□

例 1.4. Consider the compactly supported cohomology of the real line R:Hck​(R)={C0​k=1k=1​This satisfies Poincaré duality when compared against the standard cohomology of R:Hk(R)={C0​k=0k=0​

Proof. The topological space R is homeomorphic to the open interval (0,1). Because compactly supported cohomology is a topological invariant under homeomorphisms, we immediately have:Hck​(R)≅Hck​((0,1))

First, since the closed interval [0,1] is compact, its compactly supported cohomology coincides with its standard cohomology, which in turn is isomorphic to its singular cohomology. That is,Hck​([0,1])=Hk([0,1])≅Hsingk​([0,1])This yields the standard result:Hck​([0,1])={C0​if k=0,if k>0.​

注 1.5. In general, this isomorphism holds for locally contractible spaces. Here, the space is hereditarily paracompact.

Second, let X=[0,1]. Consider the closed subset Z={1} with inclusion i:Z↪X, and the open complement U=[0,1) with inclusion j:U↪X.

We apply the compactly supported cohomology functor Hck​ to the distinguished triangle associated with the constant sheaf CX​:j!​CU​=j!​j∗CX​⟶CX​⟶i∗​i∗CX​=i∗​Cpt​+1​This induces a long exact sequence in compactly supported cohomology:0⟶Hc0​([0,1))⟶H0([0,1])≅​H0(pt)⟶Hc1​([0,1))⟶H1([0,1])⟶H1(pt)⟶⋯Substituting the known cohomology groups (where H0([0,1])≅C, H0(pt)≅C, and the degree 1 terms are 0), we obtain:0⟶Hc0​([0,1))⟶C≅​C⟶Hc1​([0,1))⟶0⟶0⟶⋯Because the map between the C terms is an isomorphism, it forces the surrounding terms to vanish. Therefore, we conclude that:Hck​([0,1))=0for all k∈Z.

Third, we repeat the process by setting X=[0,1). Consider the closed subset Z={0} with inclusion i:Z↪X, and the open complement U=(0,1) with inclusion j:U↪X.

Taking the long exact sequence in compactly supported cohomology for this new decomposition yields:0⟶Hc0​((0,1))⟶Hc0​([0,1))⟶Hc0​(pt)⟶Hc1​((0,1))⟶Hc1​([0,1))⟶Hc1​(pt)⟶⋯From our previous step, we know that Hc0​([0,1))=0 and Hc1​([0,1))=0. We also know that for a point, Hc0​(pt)=C and Hc1​(pt)=0. Substituting these values into the exact sequence gives:0⟶Hc0​((0,1))⟶0⟶C≅​Hc1​((0,1))⟶0⟶0⟶⋯This sequence implies that Hc0​((0,1))=0 and Hc1​((0,1))≅C. 3Constructibility

注 3.1. Henceforth, we will assume that all spaces considered are algebraic varieties.

Recall the definition of the constructible derived category: Dcb​(X) is the full subcategory of Db(X) consisting of bounded complexes of sheaves F∙ such that for every integer k, the cohomology sheaf Hk(F∙) is a constructible sheaf.

It is an easy verification to show that Dcb​(X) is a triangulated subcategory. Specifically, it is stable under the shift functor [1] and under taking mapping cones.

Goal: Our primary goal in the upcoming sections is to show that the constructible derived category Dcb​(X) is stable under the Grothendieck six functors.

命题 3.2. Let f:X⟶Y be a morphism between algebraic varieties. For any complex F∈Dcb​(Y), the pullback f∗F belongs to Dcb​(X).

Proof. Choose a stratification Y=∐Yt​ such that for every integer k, the cohomology sheaf restricted to the stratum, Hk(F)∣Yt​​, is a finite-rank local system (i.e., it belongs to Loc(Yt​)fin).

Taking the preimages of these strata yields a stratification on X, given by X=∐f−1(Yt​)=∐Xt​ . Because the pullback of a local system is a local system and the exactness of pullback, the restriction of the cohomology of the pullback, Hk(f∗F)∣f−1(Yt​)​, belongs to Loc(f−1(Yt​))fin. This demonstrates that f∗F is constructible.

□

Proof. The proof is straightforward and left as an exercise.

定理 3.4. Let f:X⟶Y be a morphism between algebraic varieties, and let F∈Dcb​(X). Then the pushforward complexes f∗​F and f!​F both belong to Dcb​(Y).

Proof Sketch. The rigorous proof of this theorem is highly non-trivial; we refer the reader to Achar, Theorem 2.7.1. We provide a sketch of the proof below.

First, we invoke Nagata’s Compactification Theorem: for any morphism f:X⟶Y between algebraic varieties, there exists an algebraic variety X~, an open embedding j:X↪X~, and a proper morphism f~​:X~⟶Y such that: f=f~​∘j.

Since the functors compose naturally as f∗​=f~​∗​∘j∗​ and f!​=f~​!​∘j!​, it suffices to reduce the problem to verifying the statement for two specific types of morphisms: an open embedding, and a proper morphism. (Recall that for a proper morphism, f∗​=f!​).

1.

Let us consider the case where f:X↪Y is an open embedding. (In fact, this assumption can be weakened to f being a locally closed embedding).

1.

We can choose a stratification Y=∐Ys​ such that X is exactly a union of a subset of these strata, and the restriction Hk(F)∣Ys​​ belongs to Loc(Ys​)fin. For the extension by zero functor f!​, its restriction to a stratum Ys​ is given by:(f!​F)∣Ys​​={F∣Ys​​0​if Ys​⊆X,if Ys​⊆X.​In both cases, the restriction is constructible. This immediately implies that f!​F∈Dcb​(Y).

2.

To show that f∗​F is constructible, we proceed through a series of reductions:

▪

By using truncation distinguished triangles, we can reduce the problem from bounded complexes to considering a single sheaf F in the abelian category Sh(X).

▪

We can proceed by induction on the number of irreducible components of X. By applying the open-closed distinguished triangle j!​j∗F⟶F⟶i∗​i∗F+1​, we can reduce the problem to the case where X is an irreducible variety.

▪

Any morphism f:X⟶Y can be factored as X⟶f(X)​↪Y, where the second map is a proper closed embedding. This allows us to reduce to the case where f is a dominant morphism.

Choose an open embedding h:U↪X such that the restriction L=F∣U​ is a finite-rank local system, meaning L∈Loc(U)fin. Let i:Z=X∖U↪X be the closed embedding of the complement. We have the distinguished triangle: i∗​i!F⟶F⟶h∗​L+1​Applying the functor f∗​, we obtain the distinguished triangle: (f∘i)∗​i!F⟶f∗​F⟶f∗​h∗​L+1​

To analyze the term f∗​h∗​L, we need to invoke the resolution of singularities.

Suppose X is an irreducible variety and Z⊊X is a proper closed subset such that the complement X∖Z is non-empty and smooth. There exists a proper morphism π:X⟶X satisfying the following properties:

1.

The space X is a smooth variety.

2.

The restriction of π over the complement, π∣π−1(X∖Z)​:π−1(X∖Z)⟶X∖Z, is an isomorphism.

3.

The preimage π−1(Z)⊂X is a simple normal crossing divisor (which means locally it can be defined by an equation of the form ∏xi​=0).

Using this resolution, the problem reduces to a local computation for the normal crossing case, specifically an embedding of the form i:{0}×Cd↪Cn−d×Cd. From here, one applies an ad hoc argument (details of which can be found in the appendix of Achar’s book).

2.

Finally, if f is a proper morphism, one can use induction on the dimension to reduce to the case where dimY=0. In this case, we need to show that f∗​F∈Dcb​(pt), which essentially means the cohomology groups are finite-dimensional. This fact follows from the Artin Vanishing Theorem.

命题 3.6. For any complexes F,G∈Dcb​(X), the derived sheaf Hom complex RHom(F,G) belongs to Dcb​(X).

Proof. Take an open subset U with inclusion j:U↪X such that the cohomology sheaves Hk(F∣U​) and Hk(G∣U​) are finite-rank local systems (i.e., they belong to Loc(U)fin). Let i:Z=X∖U↪X denote the closed embedding of the complement.

We start with the standard open-closed distinguished triangle for F:j!​j∗F⟶F⟶i∗​i∗F+1​Applying the functor RHom(−,G), we obtain the following distinguished triangle:RHom(i∗​i∗F,G)⟶RHom(F,G)⟶RHom(j!​j∗F,G)+1​By adjunction and the projection formula, we can rewrite the outer terms. The distinguished triangle becomes:i∗​RHom(i∗F,i!G)⟶RHom(F,G)⟶j∗​RHom(F∣U​,G∣U​)+1​

We know that j∗​RHom(F∣U​,G∣U​) belongs to Dcb​(X) because F∣U​ and G∣U​ are local systems (making their internal Hom a local system) and the pushforward j∗​ preserves constructibility. Therefore, to show that the middle term RHom(F,G) is in Dcb​(X), we only need to show that the left term i∗​RHom(i∗F,i!G) is in Dcb​(X).

By an induction argument on the dimension of the space, it suffices to show that i!G∈Dcb​(Z).

To see this, consider the standard distinguished triangle for G:i∗​i!G⟶G⟶j∗​j∗G+1​By our initial assumptions, G∈Dcb​(X). Furthermore, because the functors j∗ and j∗​ preserve constructibility, the term j∗​j∗G also belongs to Dcb​(X).

Since two out of the three terms in the distinguished triangle belong to the triangulated category Dcb​(X), the third term must as well. This implies that i∗​i!G∈Dcb​(X). Finally, applying the pullback functor i∗ (which also preserves constructibility), we conclude:i∗(i∗​i!G)≃i!G∈Dcb​(Z).This completes the proof.

□
