3​ Remark

In summary, with a specific control ∂ν∂ϕ​ on Σ, we get the initial state that will evolve into the balanced state at T; when given an initial state in an attempt to find a control forcing it into the balanced state at T, the control is obtained by reversing the process. Or in other words, operating Λ is what we have already been able to do, and Λ−1 solves the problem.

The fact that Λ is invertible is a consequence of introducing the Hilbert structure F on the space of initial data. The difficulty, then, lies in establishing the uniqueness theorem. Finally, two concluding remarks are in order.

Q

Does the time T play any role in the problem?

A

Yes. Recall that as a hyperbolic system, the effect of boundary data (here the control v) is of finite propagation speed. So T has to be taken sufficiently large. (T is not big enough in the picture, as the shaded cone of dependence at (x0,T) does not touch ∂Ω×(0,T).)

Q

Are the spaces F,F′ necessarily abstract?

A

Surely not. In some cases F can be characterized explicitly. For example, if T>2x∈Ωsup​∣x−x0∣,Σ={x∈∂Ω∣(x−x0)⋅ν(x)>0}×(0,T)for an arbitrary x0∈Rn, then as is shown in [Lions 1] Chapter 1 corollary 5.1, the uniqueness theorem holds, and F=H01​(Ω)×L2(Ω),F′=H−1(Ω)×L2(Ω).Hence the problem is completely solved in this case.
