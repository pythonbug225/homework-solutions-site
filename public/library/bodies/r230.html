Question 1. Consider a group G and a set M.

•

Let G×M(g,p)​→M↦g⋅p​be a left action. Then M×G(p,g)​→M↦p∗g=g−1⋅p​defines a right action of G on M.

Find an example of a left action G×M(p,g)​→M↦p∗g=g⋅p​does not define a right action of G on M.

Question 3. Let G,H be Lie groups with a Lie group homomorphism ϕ:G→H. Suppose that G acts continuously on the right on smooth manifold M and H acts continuously on the right on smooth manifold N. Consider a ϕ-equivariant map f, i.e. f(p⋅g)=f(p)⋅ϕ(g),∀p∈M,g∈G.Prove

•

If f is continuous, then f induces a continuous map fϕ​:M/G→N/H.

•

If ϕ is an isomorphism and f is a homeomorphism, then fϕ​ is a homeomorphism.

Question 4. Consider the half-plane H={z∈C:Imz>0}and the special linear group SL(2,R)={(ac​bd​):ad−bc=1 with a,b,c,d∈R}

•

Show that the map SL(2,R)×H(A,z)​→H↦cz+daz+b​​is a well-defined left-action of SL(2,R) on H.

•

Prove that this action is transitive and that it defines a diffeomorphism between H and SL(2,R)/SO(2).

Question 5. Consider the Möbius strip M={(eιϕ,reιϕ/2):ϕ∈[0,2π],r∈[−1,1]}⊂S1×CDefine the projection π:M→S1 by π=pr1​∣M​:S1×C→S1. Denote the map fn​:S1→S1, fn​(z)=zn for n∈Z.

•

Show that π:M→S1 is a fibre bundle with general fibre [−1,1].

•

Prove that the boundary ∂M is connected and that the bundle π:M→S1 is not trivial.

•

Prove that the image of any smooth section s:S1→M intersects the zero section z:S1→M, z(α)=(α,0).

•

Show that the pull-back bundle fn∗​M is isomorphic to the bundle Mn​→S1 given by Mn​:={(eιψ,reιnψ/2):ψ∈[0,2π],r∈[−1,1]}⊂S1×C.

•

Determine those n∈Z for which fn∗​M is trivial and those for which it is non-trivial.

Question 6. Let G→P→πM be a principal bundle and f:N→M a smooth map between smooth manifolds. Prove that the pullback f∗P has the canonical structure of a principal G-bundle over N.

Question 7. Let F→E→πM and F′→E′→πM be fibre bundles and H:E→E′ a bundle morphism. Suppose that H maps every fibre of E diffeomorphically onto a fibre of E′. Show that H is a bundle isomorphism.

Question 8. Let G→P→πM and G′→P′→πM be principle bundles. Suppose f:G→G′ is a Lie group isomorphism. Show that every f-equivariant bundle morphism H:P→P′ is a diffeomorphism.

Question 9. Let E→M be a K-vector bundle of rank m with a positive definite bundle metric. Suppose F⊂E is a vector subbundle. Prove that the orthogonal complement F⊥ is a vector subbundle of E and that F⊕F⊥ is isomorphic to E.

Question 10. Let G→P→πM be a principle bundle and ρ:G→GL(V), ρi​:G→GL(Vi​) for i=1,2 representations on V,Vi​. Consider the associated vector bundles E=P×ρ​V,Ei​×ρi​​Vi​.

•

Show that the dual bundle E∗, the direct sum E1​⊕E2​ and the tensor product E1​⊗E2​ are isomorphic to vector bundles associated to P.

•

Determine the corresponding representations of G and the vector bundle isomorphisms.

Question 11. Let E→M be a complex vector bundle of rank n≥2. Show that E is associated to a principal SU(n)-bundle over M if and only if ΛnE is a trivial complex line bundle.

Question 12. Let E=P×ρ​V be an associated vector bundle and α a section of the adjoint bundle Ad(P). Prove that α defines a canonical endomorphism of the vector bundle E.
