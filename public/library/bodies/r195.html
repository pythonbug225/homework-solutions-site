定理 0.1. Suppose X is an irreducible smooth projective surface over a field k. Let L,H∈Pic(X) be line bundles such that H⋅H>0 and L⋅H=0. Then:

(a)

L⋅L≤0.

(b)

L⋅L=0 if and only if L≡num​0.

Proof. The proof proceeds in several steps.

Step 1:

引理 0.2. If H is an ample line bundle and M is a line bundle such that (M⋅H)>(ωX​⋅H), then h2(X,M)=0.

Proof of Lemma 1. By Serre duality, we have h2(X,M)=h0(X,ωX​⊗M∨). We prove this by contradiction. Suppose that h0(X,ωX​⊗M∨)=0. Then there exists an effective divisor D such that O(D)≅ωX​⊗M∨.

By the ampleness of H and the effectiveness of D, we must have (H⋅D)>0. On the other hand, since D≡ωX​−M, this implies (ωX​⋅H)>(M⋅H). This directly contradicts our initial assumption! Thus, h2(X,M)=0.

□

Step 2:

引理 0.3. If H is an ample line bundle, and L is a line bundle with L⋅L>0 and L⋅H>0, then for n≫0, the tensor power L⊗n has a non-zero global section (i.e., h0(X,L⊗n)>0).

Proof of Lemma 2. Since L⋅H>0, we can find a sufficiently large integer n≫0 such that n(L⋅H)>(ωX​⋅H). By Lemma 1, this implies h2(X,L⊗n)=0.

Applying the Riemann-Roch theorem for surfaces to L⊗n, we obtain:h0(X,L⊗n)−h1(X,L⊗n)​=χ(X,L⊗n)=2n2​(L⋅L)−2n​(L⋅ωX​)+χ(X,OX​).​Since L⋅L>0, the quadratic term 2n2​(L⋅L) strictly dominates the expression for large n. Therefore, for n≫0, the right-hand side approaches infinity, which implies h0(X,L⊗n)−h1(X,L⊗n)>0. Since h1(X,L⊗n)≥0, it immediately follows that h0(X,L⊗n)>0.

□

Step 3: We now prove the main theorem for the specific case where H is ample.

For n≫0, the line bundle L⊗H⊗n becomes ample (or very ample). Let us denote this ample line bundle by H′. We proceed to prove the theorem by contradiction. Suppose that L⋅L>0. Let D∈∣L⊗m∣ be the corresponding effective divisor. Since H is ample, its intersection with any effective divisor is strictly positive, hence (D⋅H)>0. This implies m1​(D⋅H)=(L⋅H)>0. Step 4: The case (L⋅L)=0 when H is ample. Suppose (L⋅L)=0. We wish to prove that L≡num​0. We proceed by contradiction and suppose L≡num​0. Step 5: The general case. Now suppose H is not necessarily ample, but only satisfies (H⋅H)>0. Let A be an arbitrary ample line bundle on X. If (A⋅L)=0, then by applying the established ample case to A, we immediately get (L⋅L)≤0 (and (L⋅L)=0 if and only if L≡num​0). The theorem holds. If (A⋅L)=0, then trivially L≡num​0. 引理 0.4 (Neron–Severi group). N1​(X):=Div(X)/∼, where ∼ is the numerical equivalence relation. Then N1​(X) is a free abelian group of finite rank.

推论 0.5. Let X be an irreducible smooth projective surface over a field k. Let ρ be the Picard number of X.

(a)

In the rational Néron-Severi space N1(X)Q​=(Pic(X)/≡num​)⊗Q, there exists an orthogonal basis e1​,e2​,…,eρ​ such that the intersection form is diagonal with exactly one strictly positive entry and ρ−1 strictly negative entries.

(b)

In the real Néron-Severi space N1(X)R​=N1(X)Q​⊗Q​R, there exists an orthogonal basis u1​,u2​,…,uρ​ such that the intersection matrix is exactly diag(1,−1,…,−1).

Proof. Proof of (a): The N1(X)Q​ case.

Since X is a projective surface, there exists an ample line bundle H. Let e1​=[H]∈N1(X)Q​ be its numerical class. Because H is ample, its self-intersection is strictly positive, meaning e1​⋅e1​>0.

We define the orthogonal complement of e1​ in N1(X)Q​ with respect to the intersection form:e1⊥​={D∈N1(X)Q​∣D⋅e1​=0}.Because e1​⋅e1​=0, the intersection form is non-degenerate along the one-dimensional subspace spanned by e1​. Therefore, we have an orthogonal direct sum decomposition of the ρ-dimensional vector space N1(X)Q​:N1(X)Q​=Qe1​⊕e1⊥​.The dimension of e1⊥​ is exactly ρ−1.

By the Hodge Index Theorem, for any non-zero element L∈e1⊥​, we have L⋅e1​=0 and L≡num​0. The theorem guarantees that L⋅L<0. This implies that the restriction of the intersection form to the subspace e1⊥​ is strictly negative definite.

Since every definite symmetric bilinear form over a field of characteristic zero admits an orthogonal basis (via the Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization process), there exists an orthogonal basis e2​,…,eρ​ for e1⊥​.

Therefore, {e1​,e2​,…,eρ​} forms an orthogonal basis for the entire space N1(X)Q​. Under this basis, the intersection matrix is diagonal. The diagonal entries are e1​⋅e1​>0 and ei​⋅ei​<0 for i=2,…,ρ. Thus, the signature of the intersection form is (+,−,…,−).

Proof of (b): The N1(X)R​ case.

We now extend the scalars to the real numbers, considering the space N1(X)R​. The orthogonal basis {e1​,e2​,…,eρ​} found in part (a) naturally serves as an orthogonal basis for N1(X)R​.

Over the real numbers, we can normalize these basis vectors because every positive real number has a well-defined real square root. We define a new basis {u1​,u2​,…,uρ​} by scaling the previous basis vectors:u1​=e1​⋅e1​​1​e1​,and for i=2,…,ρ,ui​=−(ei​⋅ei​)​1​ei​.We can compute the self-intersections of this new basis. For u1​, we have:u1​⋅u1​=(e1​⋅e1​​1​)2(e1​⋅e1​)=e1​⋅e1​e1​⋅e1​​=1.For i≥2, since ei​⋅ei​<0, the quantity −(ei​⋅ei​) is strictly positive, so its real square root is well-defined. We have:ui​⋅ui​=(−(ei​⋅ei​)​1​)2(ei​⋅ei​)=−(ei​⋅ei​)ei​⋅ei​​=−1.Since scaling vectors does not affect their mutual orthogonality, {u1​,…,uρ​} is an orthogonal basis for N1(X)R​. Under this normalized basis, the intersection matrix is exactly the diagonal matrix diag(1,−1,…,−1).
