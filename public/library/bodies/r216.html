1Recollection on f∗​,f∗,⊗, and Hom(−,−)

定义 1.1. Let X be a topological space, and let Sh(X) denote the category of sheaves of C-vector spaces on X. The derived category of Sh(X) is defined as D(X)=K(Sh(X))[Q−1]. We define the associated bounded derived categories as follows:

2The Functor f!​ (!-pushforward)

定义 2.1. A continuous map f:X→Y is proper if it is universally closed, that is, for any topological space Z, the product map f×idZ​:X×Z→Y×Z is closed.

1.

f is proper.

2.

3.

注 2.3. For a finite type morphism f:X→Y between algebraic varieties, the induced continuous map on complex points f:X(C)→Y(C) is proper if and only if f is a proper morphism in the sense of algebraic geometry.

4The Functor f! (!-pullback)

定义 4.1. Let f:X→Y be a continuous map. The functor ∘f!​:Sh(X)→Sh(Y) is said to have finite cohomological dimension if there exists an integer r∈N such that for all k>r and for all sheaves F∈Sh(X), we have Rk(∘f!​)F=0.

This condition is denoted by dim(∘f!​)<+∞.

例 4.2. Let X be an algebraic or analytic variety. Every sheaf F∈Sh(X) has an injective resolution of length at most 2dimX. This implies that for any continuous map f:X→Y between varieties, we always have dim(∘f!​)<+∞.

5Verdier Duality Functor

定义 5.1. The Verdier duality functor is defined as:D=DX​:D−(X)op⟶D+(X),where the action on a complex F is given byD(F)=RHom(F,ωX​).

引理 5.2. For a continuous map f:X→Y, we have the following natural isomorphisms:

1.

f∗​DF≃D(f!​F)

2.

f!DF≃D(f∗F)

Proof. Recall the Verdier duality adjunction for a map f:X→Y:RHom(f!​F,G)≃f∗​RHom(F,f!G).

(1) To prove the first isomorphism, we let G=ωY​. The left-hand side becomes RHom(f!​F,ωY​)=D(f!​F). The right-hand side becomes f∗​RHom(F,f!ωY​). Since f!ωY​≃ωX​, this is exactly f∗​DF.

(2) To prove the second isomorphism, we first need the following theorem:

定理 5.3 (Projection Formula). There is a natural isomorphism:f!​F⊗LG≃f!​(F⊗Lf∗G).(2)

The detailed proof can be found in [Kashiwara-Shapira, Proposition 2.6.6].

From the projection formula, we can deduce the following fundamental isomorphism:f!RHom(F1​,F2​)≃RHom(f∗F1​,f!F2​).(3)In fact, for any complex F, we have the following sequence of natural isomorphisms:Hom(F,f!RHom(F1​,F2​))​≃Hom(f!​F⊗LF1​,F2​)≃Hom(f!​(F⊗Lf∗F1​),F2​)≃Hom(F⊗Lf∗F1​,f!F2​)≃Hom(F,RHom(f∗F1​,f!F2​)).​Since this holds for any F, the isomorphism (3) follows.
